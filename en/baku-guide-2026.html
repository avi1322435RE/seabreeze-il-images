Baku 2026: Where East Meets West

The complete guide

OHAYON PROPERTIES | BAKU · Updated November 2026

Baku – far more than you imagined

On the shores of the Caspian Sea, where Europe meets Asia, lies Baku, the capital of Azerbaijan. It is a city of contrasts: ancient stone alleys and historic fortresses alongside skyscrapers, wide boulevards, hotels and modern residential developments.

In recent decades Baku has gone through significant urban development. It offers an intriguing travel destination, a rich culture, a varied cuisine and a chance to get to know a property market that is different from the one you may be used to.

1. The history of Baku: from port city to energy capital

Baku's story is tied to its location on the Caspian coast and to the region's oil resources. Over centuries the city grew into a centre of trade, crafts and culture.

The Old City, Icherisheher, is enclosed by walls and holds alleys, mosques, bathhouses, the Maiden Tower and the Palace of the Shirvanshahs. In 2000 it was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In the second half of the 19th century the oil industry expanded. Entrepreneurs and investors came to the city, and industrialists funded buildings and cultural institutions. After the Soviet period Azerbaijan became independent in 1991; since then the energy industry has remained a central part of its economy.

2. Modern Baku: architecture and urban development

The Flame Towers are one of the symbols of the new Baku. The three towers combine residential, hotel and office uses, and their facades are lit up in the evening.

The Heydar Aliyev Center, designed by the architect Zaha Hadid, is known for its curved lines and its spaces for culture and exhibitions.

Baku Boulevard runs along the coastline and includes walking paths, green spaces, cafés and leisure spots.

3. The Caspian Sea and Sea Breeze

Baku lies on the western shore of the Caspian Sea, the largest enclosed body of water in the world. The sea shapes the climate, tourism and urban development.

North of central Baku, in the Nardaran area, is the Sea Breeze development. It combines residential, resort, retail and leisure projects alongside plans for further development.

It is important to distinguish between completed and occupied developments and buildings that are under construction or still being planned. Presenting a future plan does not mean it has already been carried out.

4. Tourism in Baku

Baku lets you combine a city break, a historical trip, shopping, restaurants and day trips outside the city. Well-known sights include the Old City, Fountain Square, Nizami Street, Baku Boulevard, the Heydar Aliyev Center and the sites of the Absheron Peninsula.

The city also suits family visits. Summer is better for beach activities, while in winter the weather can be cold and windy.

5. Azerbaijani cuisine and the culture of hospitality

Azerbaijani cuisine combines influences from the Caucasus, Persia, Turkey and Central Asia. It is based on rice, meat, vegetables, herbs and pastries.

Plov is a festive rice dish; dolma consists of stuffed vine leaves or vegetables; qutab is a thin pastry with various fillings; and Azerbaijani kebab is sometimes served with vegetables and herbs.

Tea drinking is a central part of the culture of hospitality, and tea is often served in traditional glasses with jams and sweets.

6. The economy of Azerbaijan

Azerbaijan is an oil and gas producer and exporter in the Caspian region. Energy revenues have helped fund infrastructure and development, but they also create exposure to changes in energy prices.

The country is working to develop other sectors, including tourism, transport, logistics, trade and agriculture. Baku concentrates a large share of business activity.

Anyone looking at real estate should consider residents' incomes, the number of jobs, financing conditions, rental demand and the volume of new construction.

7. The Baku property market

The property market includes apartments in older buildings, modern residential complexes, luxury apartments and resort properties along the coast. Each area has its own patterns of demand, pricing and rental.

A city-centre apartment may appeal to local tenants and workers; a holiday apartment may depend more on tourism and seasonality.

Before buying, it is important to check the location, the price relative to similar apartments, the construction status, the registration of rights, the finish level, maintenance costs and rental options. Developer forecasts must be distinguished from verified data.

8. Israel–Azerbaijan relations

Israel and Azerbaijan have maintained diplomatic relations since 1992 and cooperate in various fields, including energy, trade, agriculture and technology.

In 2023 Azerbaijan opened an embassy in Israel. Relations between the countries are an important part of the business and cultural context, but they do not replace a legal and financial review of private transactions.

9. Why does Baku attract investors?

The geographic location, the energy industry, infrastructure development and tourism attract investors' attention. The property market offers different products: urban homes, holiday apartments and new projects. Alongside the opportunities there are risks: limited liquidity, currency fluctuations, seasonality, operating costs, registration of rights and differences between local law and the law of your home country. A sound investment starts with checking the data, not just the renderings.

10. The Baku of the future

Baku continues to change through the development of infrastructure, retail, hotels and residential districts. Along the Caspian there is a clear trend of combining homes, leisure and services in one environment.

However, handover dates, the scope of development and future plans may change. For an investor, it is important to assess each project by what already exists and what can be verified.

In summary

Baku combines a rich history, a unique culture, modern architecture and an economy based on energy alongside the development of other sectors. Whether you are planning a holiday or considering buying a property, it is worth getting to know the city and the characteristics of its market in depth.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Baku?

It is the capital of Azerbaijan, on the western shore of the Caspian Sea.

What language is spoken?

The official language is Azerbaijani; Russian is also widely spoken, and in tourist areas you can find English speakers.

What is the local currency?

The Azerbaijani manat (AZN).

Can foreigners buy an apartment?

In principle yes, subject to local law; there are various restrictions on foreign ownership of land, and the rights in each property must be checked.

Is Sea Breeze in central Baku?

No. The development is in the Nardaran area, north of the city centre.
